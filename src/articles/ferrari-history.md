**There is no name in the world of cars more famous than Ferrari. The prancing horse on a yellow shield is recognized on every continent, in every language, by people who have never driven a car. But the story behind Ferrari is not just about beautiful machines and racing victories — it is the story of one man's extraordinary, obsessive, often tragic life, and the company he built from nothing to become the most iconic automotive brand in history.**

---

## The Man: Enzo Ferrari

**Enzo Anselmo Ferrari** was born on February 18, 1898 in Modena. At just 10 years old, Enzo watched a car race for the first time — the 1908 Circuito di Bologna — and something changed inside him forever.

After surviving World War I and a devastating influenza epidemic, Enzo joined **Alfa Romeo** in **1920**. He won races, including the **Coppa Acerbo** in 1924, but his greatest talent was in organization and leadership. In **1929**, Enzo founded **Scuderia Ferrari** as the official racing team for Alfa Romeo.

---

## The Prancing Horse: A Symbol Born in Battle

In **1923**, after winning a race at Ravenna, Enzo met the parents of **Francesco Baracca** — a WWI flying ace who had painted a black prancing horse on his aircraft. Baracca's mother told Enzo: take this symbol, put it on your cars. Enzo placed the black horse on a **yellow shield** — yellow being the official color of Modena. It has been Ferrari's badge ever since.

---

## 1939–1947: Breaking Free and Starting Over

In **1939**, Enzo broke away from Alfa Romeo and founded **Auto Avio Costruzioni**. After WWII, he relocated to **Maranello** — where Ferrari's factory stands to this day.

In **1947**, **Ferrari S.p.A.** was officially founded. The first car — the **125 S** — was powered by a **1.5-litre V12 engine**. Of the ten races it entered that year, it won six. The Ferrari era had begun.

---

## 1948–1957: Le Mans, Formula 1, and the Birth of a Legend

In **1949**, Ferrari won the **24 Hours of Le Mans**. In **1950**, Ferrari entered the inaugural Formula 1 season — and has competed in **every single F1 season since**, the only team in history to do so. The first F1 championships came in **1952 and 1953** with **Alberto Ascari**.

In **1956**, Enzo's son **Dino** died from muscular dystrophy at just 24. Enzo was shattered. He never fully recovered. In Dino's honor, Ferrari named a range of V6-engined road cars the **Dino** — a legacy that lives on today in the Ferrari 296.

---

## The Icons: 250 GTO and the Road Car Revolution

The **Ferrari 250 GTO** (1962) is considered the greatest Ferrari ever made. Today it sells for **$50–70 million** at auction. The **275 GTB**, the **Daytona**, the **Dino 246 GT** — through the 1960s and 1970s, Ferrari defined what an Italian supercar should look, feel, and sound like.

---

## 1963: Ferrari vs. Ford — The Greatest Rivalry in Racing History

Ford approached Enzo about a takeover. At the last moment, Enzo walked away when he learned Ford would control Ferrari's racing division. Henry Ford II was furious and vowed to beat Ferrari at Le Mans. The result was the **[Ford GT40](/2026/02/12/ford-gt-history/)**. The rivalry — the subject of the 2019 film **Ford v Ferrari** — remains the most dramatic chapter in endurance racing history.

---

## 1969: Fiat Takes Control

In **1969**, Enzo sold **50% of Ferrari to Fiat**. Fiat's resources allowed Ferrari to develop faster and invest in new technology. Enzo retained control of Scuderia Ferrari until his death.

---

## 1975–1979: Lauda, Villeneuve, and Formula 1 Glory

**Niki Lauda** won F1 titles in **1975** and **1977**. **Gilles Villeneuve** became the most beloved Ferrari driver of all time before being killed at the 1982 Belgian Grand Prix. **Jody Scheckter** won in **1979** — the last Ferrari title for 21 years.

---

## The F40, the Enzo, and the Modern Era

In **1987**, Enzo commissioned the **[Ferrari F40](/2026/01/14/ferrari-f40/)**. Twin-turbo V8, 478hp, carbon fiber and Kevlar. The first production car to break **200 mph**.

**Enzo Ferrari died on August 14, 1988**, at the age of 90. Under his leadership, Ferrari won over **5,000 races** and **25 World Championships**.

In **1995**, the **[Ferrari F50](/2026/01/14/ferrari-f50/)** brought Formula 1 technology directly to the road with a naturally aspirated V12 derived from F1. In **2002**, Ferrari honored its founder with the **[Ferrari Enzo](/2026/01/17/ferrari-enzo/)** — 660hp V12, 400 units, worth over $3 million today.

---

## 2000–2004: Schumacher and the Greatest Dynasty in F1 History

**Michael Schumacher** and **Ross Brawn** delivered **five consecutive Drivers' and Constructors' Championships** from 2000 to 2004. Schumacher won 72 races in a Ferrari. The red cars were simply unbeatable.

---

## 2013–Present: LaFerrari, the SF90, and the F80

The **[LaFerrari](/2026/01/15/laferrari-hybrid-hypercar/)** (2013) produced **963 horsepower** from a V12 + electric motor. 499 units. It became one third of the legendary [Hypercar Holy Trinity](/2026/02/12/hypercar-holy-trinity/) alongside the McLaren P1 and Porsche 918 Spyder. The **[SF90 Stradale](/2026/01/15/ferrari-sf90-stradale/)** (2019) reached 1,000hp — Ferrari's first plug-in hybrid. The track-focused **[SF90 XX Stradale](/2026/03/01/ferrari-sf90-xx-stradale/)** pushed it to 1,016hp. And in 2024, the **[Ferrari F80](/2026/03/01/ferrari-f80/)** — 1,200hp V6 hybrid, 0–62 in 2.15 seconds. The fastest Ferrari ever built.

The **[488 Pista](/2026/01/15/ferrari-488-pista/)** redefined what a track-focused mid-engine Ferrari could do, while the one-off **[P80/C](/2026/01/15/ferrari-p80c/)** showed Ferrari's bespoke capabilities. And in 2026, the **[Ferrari Amalfi Spider](/2026/03/22/ferrari-amalfi-spider/)** brings the joy of open-top V8 driving back to the range.

In Formula 1, **Charles Leclerc** and **Carlos Sainz** kept Ferrari competitive, returning to genuine championship contention.

## Why Ferrari Is Different

No company has ever matched Ferrari's combination of racing history, emotional intensity, visual beauty, and cultural meaning. The [rivalry with Lamborghini](/2026/02/12/ferrari-vs-lamborghini/) that Ferruccio started in the 1960s only made both brands stronger. A Ferrari is the sound of a V12 at 9,000 rpm. It is Gilles Villeneuve fighting a lost battle at the French Grand Prix. It is the red of Maranello that children point at on posters on their bedroom walls.

Enzo Ferrari built road cars reluctantly, as a way to fund his real passion. Those road cars became the most desired objects in the world. And the racing team he built with obsession and grief and genius became the most famous team in the history of sport.

That is the story of Ferrari. That is the story of the prancing horse.
