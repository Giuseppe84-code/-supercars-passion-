**McLaren is one of the most famous names in the world of cars and motorsport. But how did a young New Zealander with a dream create one of the greatest racing teams and supercar manufacturers on the planet? This is the story of McLaren — told simply, from the very beginning to today.**

---

## The Beginning: A Boy From New Zealand

The story of McLaren starts with one man: **Bruce McLaren**.

Born in Auckland, New Zealand in 1937, Bruce grew up surrounded by cars at his parents' garage. At just 14 years old he entered his first hillclimb race. By 22 he had won a Formula 1 Grand Prix — making him the youngest Grand Prix winner in history at the time.

Bruce was not just fast. He was also brilliant at understanding how cars worked and how to make them better. He had a dream: to build his own racing cars. In **1963**, he made that dream real by founding **Bruce McLaren Motor Racing**.

---

## 1963–1970: Building an Empire

From the very first day, McLaren was about winning.

The team built their first racing car — the M1A — in 1964. McLaren then entered the famous **Can-Am Series** in North America. **McLaren won five consecutive Can-Am championships from 1967 to 1971** — dominating the series so completely that their orange cars became the most feared machines in motorsport.

In **1966**, McLaren entered Formula 1 with their own car for the first time. Two years later, in 1968, **Bruce McLaren himself took the team's first Formula 1 victory** in Belgium.

In June **1970**, Bruce McLaren was killed while testing a Can-Am car at Goodwood circuit in England. He was just 32 years old. The team he built was devastated. But they did what Bruce would have wanted. They went back to the factory. They kept racing. And they kept winning.

---

## 1974–1991: World Champions, Legends, and Icons

After Bruce's death, McLaren rebuilt — and became even more successful.

In **1974**, McLaren won their first Formula 1 World Championship double with **Emerson Fittipaldi**. In 1976, **James Hunt** won the World Championship by a single point from [Ferrari](/2026/03/01/ferrari-history/)'s Niki Lauda.

Then came **Ron Dennis**. In 1980, his Project 4 Racing team merged with McLaren. Designer **John Barnard** introduced [carbon fiber](/2026/02/12/hypercar-materials-technology/) to racing for the first time with the MP4/1.

The 1980s became McLaren's golden decade. With **Niki Lauda**, **Alain Prost**, and the legendary **Ayrton Senna**, McLaren dominated Formula 1. Between 1984 and 1991, McLaren won seven World Championships. In 1988 alone, Senna and Prost won 15 of the 16 races — the most dominant season in Formula 1 history.

---

## 1992: The McLaren F1 — The Greatest Car Ever Made?

Designer **Gordon Murray** — who would later build the [T.50](/2026/02/12/gordon-murray-t50/) under his own name — had a dream: to build the greatest road car the world had ever seen.

The result was the **McLaren F1**, unveiled in 1992. A **6.1-litre BMW V12** engine. A central driving position. A top speed of **240 mph**. A dry weight of just 1,138 kg. Carbon fiber everywhere.

Only **106 examples** were ever built. Today, the McLaren F1 sells for between $15 million and $20 million at auction — making it one of the most [valuable collector cars](/2026/02/12/supercar-investment-guide/) in existence.

In **1995**, the F1 GTR racing version won the **24 Hours of Le Mans** on its first attempt — finishing first, third, fourth, fifth and thirteenth overall.

---

## 2004: The Mercedes-Benz SLR McLaren

McLaren collaborated with **Mercedes-Benz** to create the **SLR McLaren** in 2004. A dramatic grand tourer with a supercharged V8 producing 617 horsepower and a top speed of 208 mph. It was impressive, but it was not a true McLaren. The real successor to the F1 was still to come.

---

## 2010: McLaren Automotive is Born

In **2010**, McLaren launched **McLaren Automotive** as a dedicated road car manufacturer. The **McLaren 12C** arrived in 2011 — a twin-turbo V8 mid-engine supercar that immediately challenged [Ferrari](/2026/03/01/ferrari-history/) and [Lamborghini](/2026/03/01/lamborghini-history/).

---

## 2013: The McLaren P1 — Hybrid Hypercar Pioneer

The **[McLaren P1](/2026/02/12/mclaren-p1/)** was unveiled in 2013. A twin-turbo V8 combined with an electric motor produced **903 horsepower**. [Active aerodynamics](/2026/02/12/active-aerodynamics-explained/) generated up to 600 kg of downforce. Only **375 examples** were built. Already the P1 has tripled in value since new.

Together with the [LaFerrari](/2026/01/15/laferrari-hybrid-hypercar/) and [Porsche 918 Spyder](/2026/02/12/porsche-918-spyder/), the P1 formed the legendary [Hypercar Holy Trinity](/2026/02/12/hypercar-holy-trinity/) — three cars that proved hybrid technology could enhance, not diminish, the supercar experience.

---

## 2015–2020: A Supercar for Everyone

**Sports Series** — accessible supercars starting around $185,000. The 570S became one of the most praised driver's cars of its generation, and the **[600LT](/2026/01/16/mclaren-600lt/)** took its lightweight Longtail philosophy to the extreme with 592hp and 100kg stripped from the 570S.

**Super Series** — the core McLaren range. The **720S** arrived in 2017 with 710 horsepower.

**Ultimate Series** — the most extreme cars. The **[Senna](/2026/01/15/mclaren-senna/)** — 800hp with more downforce than a GT3 race car, named after the great Ayrton. The **[Speedtail](/2026/02/12/mclaren-speedtail/)** — a 250mph hyper GT with a central driving seat, the spiritual successor to the F1. And the **Elva** — a roofless, windshieldless experience machine.

---

## 2024: The McLaren W1 — The P1's Successor

In October 2024, McLaren unveiled the **[McLaren W1](/2026/03/01/mclaren-w1/)**. **1,275 horsepower** from a new twin-turbo V8 and electric motor. Rear-wheel drive only. Ground-effect aerodynamics. **2,205 pounds of downforce** in Race mode. All 399 examples — priced at $2.1 million each — were sold before the car was officially revealed.

---

## McLaren in Formula 1 Today

In **2025**, **Lando Norris** won the Drivers' World Championship — McLaren's first since Lewis Hamilton in 2008 — and the team won the Constructors' Championship for the first time since **1998**. The [technology transfer from F1 to road cars](/2026/01/14/f1-technology-in-hypercars/) has never been stronger.

McLaren has now won **13 Drivers' Championships** and **10 Constructors' Championships** in Formula 1 — second only to Ferrari in the all-time rankings.

## Why McLaren Matters

McLaren is the story of a young man from New Zealand who refused to accept limits — and built something that outlasted his own life by decades.

Every McLaren road car carries the DNA of Formula 1. Every supercar they build is designed with the same obsession for lightness, precision, and driver engagement that made their racing cars great. And every time a McLaren crosses a finish line first, it is a tribute to Bruce McLaren and the dream he never stopped chasing.
