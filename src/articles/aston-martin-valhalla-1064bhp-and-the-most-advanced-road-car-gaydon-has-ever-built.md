Aston Martin has flirted with the mid-engined supercar idea before, but the **Valhalla** is the moment the British marque goes all in. Billed as the most advanced road car Aston Martin has ever produced, it fuses Formula 1-grade thinking with everyday usability — and the numbers are seriously spicy.

### A V8 with three electric helpers

At the Valhalla's core sits a 4.0-litre twin-turbocharged V8, but it does not work alone. Three electric motors join the party, lifting combined output to around 1,064bhp and 1,100Nm of torque. The result is a hybrid powertrain that can unleash savage acceleration when you want it, glide silently on electric power when you do not, and use its motors to fill in any gap while the turbos spool. This is performance with intelligence baked in.

### Built like a racing car

The Valhalla is wrapped around a carbon-fibre monocoque — the same lightweight, ultra-rigid construction principle used in top-tier motorsport. Add active aerodynamics that adjust on the move to generate downforce where and when it is needed, and you have a car engineered to be genuinely track-ready while remaining fully road legal. It is the rare supercar designed to be exploited, not just admired.

### Why the Valhalla matters for Aston Martin

For a brand more often associated with elegant front-engined grand tourers and a certain fictional British spy, the Valhalla is a leap into new territory. It signals that Aston Martin intends to compete head-on with the mid-engined hybrid elite — the Ferraris, McLarens and Lamborghinis of the world — rather than sit politely on the sidelines. It also showcases the technology and know-how that will trickle into future Aston Martins.

### The new face of the hybrid supercar

The Valhalla lands at a fascinating moment. Combustion is reaching its engineering ceiling, and hybridisation is no longer a compromise — it is a performance multiplier. Cars like the Valhalla prove that electrification, done right, makes a supercar faster, smarter and more thrilling, not less. With its mix of V8 drama and electric precision, the Valhalla may well be remembered as the car that finally announced Aston Martin as a serious supercar player.
