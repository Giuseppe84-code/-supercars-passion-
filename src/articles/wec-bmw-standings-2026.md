The 2026 World Endurance Championship was supposed to be a fight between the manufacturers with the deepest Hypercar programmes. Instead, with the season past its midpoint, Robin Frijns and René Rast lead the drivers' standings in the No. 20 BMW M Team WRT entry, and BMW leads the manufacturers' championship.

## How the class got this crowded

The current regulations allow two routes in: Le Mans Hypercar, where a manufacturer builds essentially everything, and LMDh, where the chassis comes from one of four approved constructors and the manufacturer supplies the engine and bodywork. The second route is cheaper, and it is the reason the grid now holds entries from Genesis to Peugeot, with Ford arriving in 2027.

The conventional expectation was that the bespoke LMH cars would hold the advantage. Balance of Performance is designed to prevent exactly that, and this season it appears to be working as intended.

## Consistency over pace

BMW's position has not come from dominant single results. It has come from finishing, repeatedly, in the places that pay. Over a season measured across six-hour races and one twenty-four-hour race, the team that avoids the expensive afternoon usually ends up in front.

Whether that holds through the closing rounds is a different question. Championships decided on consistency tend to be vulnerable to a single bad race, and BMW has not yet had one.
