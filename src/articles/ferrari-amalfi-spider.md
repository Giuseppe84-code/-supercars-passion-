Ferrari has just pulled the covers off one of its most anticipated models of 2026 — the Amalfi Spider. It's the open-top version of the Amalfi coupé that replaced the Roma last year, and it arrives with something rare in the modern Ferrari lineup: a sense of pure, uncomplicated joy.

No hybrid system. No electric motors. No 1,000+ horsepower headline. Just a twin-turbocharged V8, the sky above you, and some of the finest chassis engineering Maranello has ever put into a grand tourer.

## The Numbers

At the heart of the Amalfi Spider sits Ferrari's proven 3.9-litre twin-turbocharged V8 from the F154 family — the same engine architecture that has powered some of the greatest modern Ferraris. In this application, it produces 640 CV (631 horsepower) and 760 Nm (561 lb-ft) of torque, a modest but meaningful upgrade over the Roma Spider it replaces.

The improvements come from revised turbochargers spinning up to 171,000 rpm, a new engine control unit, lighter camshafts, and a raised redline of 7,600 rpm. Power reaches the rear wheels through an 8-speed dual-clutch transmission — no all-wheel-drive here, which is exactly the point. This is a driver's GT, not a launch-control drag strip weapon.

The result: 0-100 km/h in 3.3 seconds (3.4 for the Spider due to a slight weight increase) and a top speed of 320 km/h. Fast enough to make any coastal road feel like a private racetrack.

## The Roof — A 13.5-Second Transformation

The defining feature of the Spider is, of course, the roof. Ferrari opted for a lightweight Z-fold soft top rather than a retractable hardtop — a decision that saved significant weight and allowed the designers to preserve the coupé's elegant proportions almost unchanged.

The mechanism operates in just 13.5 seconds and can be activated at speeds up to 60 km/h. The five-layer acoustic fabric provides thermal and sound insulation that Ferrari claims matches its retractable hardtop standards. With the roof up, the silhouette is virtually identical to the coupé. With it down, two sculpted buttresses behind the rear seats give the car a completely different personality.

## Design — Named After the Coast, Shaped by the Wind

The Amalfi Spider takes its name from one of the world's most celebrated driving roads — the Amalfi Coast, a serpentine stretch of tarmac carved into the cliffs above the Gulf of Salerno. And the car lives up to the name.

Every exterior surface has been refined for aerodynamic efficiency. A dynamic three-position rear wing adjusts automatically based on speed and driving mode, providing variable downforce without compromising the car's clean lines. Ferrari's engineers managed to achieve aerodynamic performance on par with the coupé — no small feat for a convertible.

The launch color is Rosso Tramonto, a deep orange-red inspired by sunsets over the Mediterranean. It's a bold choice that perfectly captures the car's character: warm, dramatic, and distinctly Italian.

## Inside — The Return of Physical Controls

One of the most praised aspects of the Amalfi platform is what Ferrari *removed*: the Roma's touchscreen-heavy interface. The Amalfi Spider features a new steering wheel with physical buttons, a return to tactile controls for climate and driving modes, and a dual-cockpit layout that puts the driver firmly in command.

Three digital displays provide all the information you need, but the critical functions — the ones you reach for while pushing through a tight corner — are all physical. It's a philosophy shift that acknowledges what enthusiasts have been saying for years: not everything needs a touchscreen.

A retractable wind deflector reduces cabin turbulence at speed, making open-top cruising genuinely comfortable rather than just tolerable.

## Technology — Brake-by-Wire and SSC 6.1

Underneath the elegant body, the Amalfi Spider packs Ferrari's latest chassis technology. The new brake-by-wire system replaces traditional mechanical braking components with an electronic system that translates pedal pressure into braking force via a central computer. The result is more precise modulation, better feel, and reduced weight.

Ferrari's Side Slip Control 6.1 is the latest evolution of the system that manages traction, stability, and the electronic differential. Combined with the brake-by-wire ABS Evo system, it creates a safety net that's sophisticated enough to let you push hard while keeping things predictable.

## Price and Availability

The Ferrari Amalfi coupé starts at approximately €240,000 in Europe ($283,000 in the US). The Spider commands a premium of around €30,000-€35,000, placing the starting price at roughly €270,000 (approximately $285,000-$300,000 in the US). UK pricing starts at around £210,000.

Deliveries are expected to begin in Q2 2026 for Europe and the UK, with US deliveries following shortly after. A fully optioned car with Tailor Made personalization will comfortably exceed €300,000 — and given Ferrari's allocation system, most buyers won't have much choice about adding options.

## The Verdict — Why This Ferrari Matters

In a world where every new hypercar seems to need four-figure horsepower and a hybrid system to justify its existence, the Amalfi Spider is refreshingly honest. It's a beautifully engineered V8 grand tourer that doesn't try to be the fastest, the most powerful, or the most technically advanced Ferrari on sale.

Instead, it focuses on what makes a convertible special: the connection between driver, machine, and the open road. The V8 soundtrack without a roof. The Italian sun on your face. The feeling that the car was designed for exactly this moment, on exactly this kind of road.

For anyone who's ever driven the Amalfi Coast — or dreamed of it — this is the Ferrari that was made for that road. And for the rest of us, it's a reminder that not every great car needs to chase records. Sometimes, it just needs to make you feel alive.
