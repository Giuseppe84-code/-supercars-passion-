The Rimac Nevera isn’t “fast for an EV.” It’s **fast, full stop**—and it achieves that with a mix of absurd power, relentless traction, and software control that feels closer to aerospace than automotive. Built by Rimac Automobili, the Nevera set out to prove something simple: **electric performance doesn’t have to be a compromise**—it can be the new benchmark.

And when you look at the numbers, it’s hard to argue.

---

## The headline specs (the ones that punch you in the chest)

From Rimac’s own published technical figures, the Nevera delivers:

* **Power:** **1,408 kW / 1,914 hp**
* **Motor torque:** **2,340 Nm**
* **Wheel torque:** **13,430 Nm**
* **Top speed:** **412 km/h (258 mph)**
* **0–100 km/h:** **1.81 s**
* **0–300 km/h:** **9.22 s**
* **¼ mile (402 m):** **8.25 s**
* **WLTP range:** **490 km** (Rimac notes this is based on final testing)

Those figures aren’t just “marketing fast.” They’re a statement that the hypercar world has been forced to respond to.

---

## Quad-motor AWD: the real magic isn’t the horsepower—it’s the control

A big number is easy to print. The hard part is **deploying it**.

The Nevera’s four-motor setup (one per wheel) makes it a different kind of performance machine: instead of relying on mechanical differentials and traction systems that react after slip, it can **control torque distribution with extreme precision**—effectively shaping how the car rotates, grips, and launches.

That’s why the Nevera is not only brutally quick in a straight line, but also shockingly stable at speed—because the drivetrain is *actively managing* the car’s attitude, not merely powering it.

---

## Battery and structure: performance starts with the pack

This is not a generic “big battery in the floor.” Rimac Technology describes the Nevera’s battery as a **structural pack integrated into the monocoque**, designed to store **120 kWh** while also contributing to stiffness.

Why that matters:

* **High power delivery** without constant thermal collapse
* **Repeatable performance**, not just one hero run
* **Stiff platform**, which helps both handling and aero consistency

---

## Charging: built to actually be usable

Rimac’s published technical specs list:

* **On-board charging:** **3-phase 22 kW**
* **Fast charging:** up to **500 kW**

For a hypercar, that’s a big deal: it means the Nevera is engineered not just to *perform*, but to *operate* in the real world of charging infrastructure.

---

## The record run that made the world pay attention

In May 2023, Rimac announced the Nevera set **23 performance records in a single day**, including a **0–400–0 km/h time of 29.93 seconds** (Rimac’s wording: “undisputed hypercar champion” for that test).

Even if you don’t obsess over records, 0–400–0 is the kind of test that exposes everything:

* acceleration
* aero
* stability
* braking
* thermal management

Doing it once is impressive. Doing it as part of a record suite is a flex.

---

## Ownership confidence: the warranty move that surprised people

In 2024, Rimac announced a new **8-year battery warranty with unlimited mileage** for Nevera buyers (also applied retroactively to cars already delivered).

That matters because it signals something rare in the hypercar segment: **confidence in the long-term durability** of the technology, not just its peak output.

---

## Production and price: ultra-rare, ultra-expensive, ultra-serious

When Rimac began series production, TechCrunch reported a **$2.5 million** price point for the Nevera.  
It’s the kind of figure that doesn’t need justification—but the engineering behind the car makes it clear what you’re paying for: not “luxury,” but **a rolling proof-of-concept that happens to be street legal**.

---

## Why the Nevera matters (even if you’re a hardcore ICE purist)

The Nevera forced a new reality:

* EVs can dominate at hypercar level without excuses
* software + torque control is now a core performance advantage
* the old “traction limit” conversation has changed (because torque delivery can be shaped per wheel)

It doesn’t replace the drama of a screaming V12. It replaces the idea that drama is required for greatness.
