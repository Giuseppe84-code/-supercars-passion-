*Because “Godzilla” didn’t just come in one flavor—some versions were basically mythical from day one.*

Every generation of GT-R has its icons, but a handful of special editions sit in a different universe: ultra-low production numbers, unique factory engineering, and values that have gone from “expensive” to “don’t even ask.”

Below are five of the rarest GT-R-related versions/specs (and one famously scarce factory color run) that most enthusiasts will only experience through photos, auction results, and late-night forum rabbit holes.

---

## 1) NISMO R34 GT-R Z-Tune — The ultimate factory “final boss”

If there’s one Skyline GT-R variant that feels like it was built to end the conversation, it’s the Z-Tune.

What makes it special isn’t only the power—it's the idea: **a factory-built “best possible” R34**, created after regular production ended.

### Why it’s so rare

Depending on the source, you’ll see **19 or 20** discussed, with several accounts pointing to **19 completed cars** (including prototypes/preserved examples), which is why totals can look inconsistent across articles and registries.

### What made it different (the *feel*, not just the stats)

* Built with obsessive attention at Omori (Nissan/NISMO’s performance HQ culture), aiming for a *complete* package: cooling, chassis, aero, braking, durability.
* Engine is widely described as an RB26-based “RB28” style build with around **500 PS** in period references, and upgraded turbos/hardware to match the intent.

### Why collectors go insane

* It’s the “factory-restomod” before factory restomods were a trend.
* Record-breaking money has been attached to the name. Multiple reports cite a **headline figure around $1.985M** connected to a Z-Tune sale narrative, even if the broader market varies wildly depending on provenance/spec/history.

---

## 2) NISMO R33 GT-R 400R — The 1990s unicorn with Le Mans DNA

Before “hypercar prices” infected JDM, the 400R was already a monster: wider, sharper, and tuned with a motorsport mindset.

### Why it’s so rare

* **Only 44** are widely cited, with multiple reputable enthusiast/market sources repeating that number.  
  (Some claim fewer verified today, which matters a lot for collectors.)

### Why it’s legendary

* It represents peak “Group A spirit” era engineering translated into a road car.
* It’s a halo for the R33 generation, which is still underrated compared to the R32/R34 hype cycle.

### What it’s worth today (proof the world has changed)

Auction and market tracking has put real weight behind the hype:

* A Japanese auction was reported around **130,000,000 yen** for a 400R (roughly **NZ$1.49m** in that report).
* Market databases have recorded **top sales near $995,000** (depending on sale/event).

---

## 3) R33 GT-R LM Limited — 188 cars, one color, pure nerd gold

The LM Limited is the kind of special edition collectors love because it’s subtle—but *real*.

### Why it’s so rare

* **188 units total**, often broken down as **86 standard** + **102 V-Spec**.

### What makes it cool (and often misunderstood)

* It commemorates Nissan’s Le Mans effort era and ties into the R33 LM story.
* It’s famous for **Champion Blue (BT2)** being effectively “the LM Limited identity.”

This is the kind of car that doesn’t need crazy numbers—its value is in authenticity, rarity, and historical context.

---

## 4) R34 GT-R Nür — The last-of-the-line “final evolution” (1,003 total)

The Nür is important because it’s not a “sticker package.” It’s a meaningful end-of-era mechanical refinement.

### Why it’s so rare

* Nissan heritage sources commonly cite **~1,000 built** across the Nür variants, with breakdowns by V-Spec II Nür and M-Spec Nür adding up to just over a thousand (often cited as **1,003**).

### What made it special

* Nür models are associated with an RB26 evolution linked to N1-style durability improvements (e.g., turbo durability changes) and “final form” details like the 300 km/h speedometer and unique trim cues.

### Market reality

Limited final editions tend to be collector magnets, and Nür-badged cars are heavily watched at major auction houses.

---

## 5) Midnight Purple III (LX0) — Not a “version,” but a factory rarity that changes everything

You asked for **Midnight Purple III**, and it deserves its own slot because it creates a rarity layer even on “normal” trims.

### Why it’s so rare

Production counts vary depending on registry methodology, but dedicated GT-R registry research and production lists commonly cite **around 198 cars** finished in **LX0 Midnight Purple III**, with a commonly repeated subset count for V-Spec examples.

### Why it matters

* Color is one of the strongest multipliers in collector value **when the color is factory, documented, and scarce**.
* On an R34 GT-R, *LX0 on the build plate* instantly tells a story.

---

# Why these “never-see-in-person” GT-Rs are worth knowing

Because they show *how Nissan and* NISMO *thought about performance at the extremes*—and how the legend keeps evolving long after the last Skyline GT-R rolled off the line.

These cars also prove something uncomfortable: **the GT-R world is now a serious collector market**, not just a tuner culture playground. Even mainstream market tracking acknowledges seven-figure Skyline territory in recent years.

---

## Buyer mindset: how collectors separate “real” from “replica”

If you’re hunting rare GT-R history (or just browsing listings for fun), the checklist is always:

* **Documentation & provenance** (auction sheets, service history, ownership chain)
* **Matching plates/IDs**
* **Factory options vs later conversions**
* **Registry confirmation when available**
* **Rust/repairs** (Japan cars can be immaculate—or expertly hidden projects)
