**Aston Martin is more than a car brand. It is a symbol of British elegance, racing heritage, and a certain kind of sophisticated cool that no other manufacturer has ever quite replicated. From a small workshop in West London in 1913 to James Bond's most famous prop and one of the most desirable names in the world — this is the complete story of Aston Martin.**

---

## The Beginning: A Hill Climb and a Name

On **January 15, 1913**, **Lionel Martin** and **Robert Bamford** founded **Bamford & Martin Ltd** in West Kensington, London. The name "Aston Martin" combined Martin's surname with the **Aston Hill Climb** near Aston Clinton in Buckinghamshire, where Lionel regularly competed. Their first car — the **"Coal Scuttle"** — was built in 1915.

---

## The Early Racing Years: 1919–1947

Through the 1920s, Aston Martin built a reputation for light, nimble, fast machines in hillclimbs and endurance events. But finances were brutal. After Lionel Martin departed in 1926, the company changed hands multiple times and survived bankruptcy.

In **1946**, industrialist **David Brown** paid **£20,500** for the entire company. Everything changed.

---

## The David Brown Era: The DB Legacy (1947–1972)

David Brown also bought **Lagonda** for its W.O. Bentley-designed engine. The **"DB"** initials on every Aston Martin stood for his name. The **DBR1** won the **24 Hours of Le Mans in 1959** — Aston Martin's greatest racing victory.

---

## 1963: The DB5 — The Most Famous Car in the World

The **DB5** (1963): 4.0L straight-six, 282hp, styled by Touring Superleggera. Then Sean Connery drove the silver DB5 in **Goldfinger** (1964). Ejector seat, machine guns, revolving number plates. It became the most recognizable car on the planet.

Fewer than **900 DB5s** were built. Today, pristine examples sell for over **$1.5 million**. A Goldfinger DB5 sold in 2019 for **$6.4 million**.

---

## 1969–1987: Financial Struggles

David Brown sold the company in 1972. Aston Martin survived another bankruptcy, changing hands multiple times. The **V8** kept the brand alive and appeared in **The Living Daylights** (1987).

---

## 1987–2007: The Ford Era

**Ford** took full ownership in 1994. The **DB7** (1994) became the best-selling Aston Martin with over **7,000 examples**. The **DB9** (2003) and **Vanquish** followed. Ford sold Aston Martin in 2007 for **£479 million**.

---

## 2009–Present: One-77, Valkyrie, and the Modern Era

The **One-77** (2009): 7.3L NA V12, 750hp, 77 units, £1.15 million each. The **Valkyrie** (2021): Cosworth V12 revving to 11,100rpm, **1,160hp**, 1,000kg. The **Valhalla** (2024): 1,000+hp hybrid.

Aston Martin returned to **Formula 1** in 2021 with **Lawrence Stroll's** backing, **Sebastian Vettel** and **Fernando Alonso** behind the wheel.

## Why Aston Martin Is Different

Aston Martin is elegance. A quiet, understated, deeply British kind of excellence. It is the car James Bond chose not because it was the fastest, but because it was the most perfectly suited to a man of taste and danger.

Over **110 years**, Aston Martin has survived seven bankruptcies, more ownership changes than almost any other manufacturer, two World Wars, and the relentless pressure of competing against companies with 10 times the resources. It has survived because the cars it builds are simply irreplaceable.
