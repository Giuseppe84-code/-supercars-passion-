**Few cars in history have achieved the legendary status of the Nissan Skyline GT-R. Known around the world as "Godzilla," it is the car that proved Japan could build a sports car to rival — and beat — the best from Europe and America. This is the complete story of the Nissan Skyline, from a modest family sedan in 1957 to one of the most iconic performance machines ever made.**

---

## The Beginning: Prince Motor Company and the First Skyline (1957)

The Nissan Skyline did not start life as a Nissan at all.

In **April 1957**, the **Prince Motor Company** launched the original Skyline as a comfortable, practical four-door sedan powered by a modest 1.5-litre engine. In **1964**, they introduced the **Prince Skyline GT** — a performance version powered by a 2.0-litre inline-six engine producing 125 horsepower.

In **1966**, Prince Motor Company merged with **Nissan**, and the Skyline became a Nissan.

---

## 1969: The First Skyline GT-R — "Hakosuka"

Launched in **February 1969**, the first GT-R was powered by a **2.0-litre DOHC inline-six** engine producing **160 horsepower**. The Hakosuka won **52 consecutive races** in Japanese domestic touring car races. Just over **2,000 first-generation GT-Rs** were built.

---

## 1973: The Rarest GT-R — "Kenmeri"

The 1973 oil crisis killed it. Only **197 Kenmeri GT-Rs** were ever built — one of the rarest production cars in Japanese history. The GT-R name then disappeared for **16 years**.

---

## 1989: The Return of Godzilla — The R32

At its heart was the legendary **RB26DETT**: a 2.6-litre twin-turbocharged inline-six engine. Official power: **280 horsepower** (substantially more in reality). The **ATTESA E-TS** all-wheel-drive system and **Super HICAS** four-wheel steering made it revolutionary.

It won the **Japanese Touring Car Championship for four consecutive years**, winning **29 consecutive races**. It won the **Bathurst 1000** in 1991 and 1992. The Australian press gave it the nickname **"Godzilla"**.

---

## 1995: The R33 — Faster, More Refined

The R33 lapped the **Nürburgring Nordschleife** in **7 minutes 59 seconds** — no production car had ever come close. Sony's *Gran Turismo* (1997) introduced an entire generation to the GT-R. A special **LM Limited** edition celebrated Nissan's return to Le Mans.

---

## 1998: The Legend — The R34

The **R34 Skyline GT-R** featured a **six-speed Getrag manual gearbox**, carbon fiber rear diffuser, and the most advanced ATTESA AWD system yet. It lapped the Nürburgring in **7 minutes 38 seconds**.

The R34 appeared in **The Fast and the Furious** driven by Paul Walker, making it the ultimate fantasy car for a generation. Production ended in **2002** with just over **11,000 R34 GT-Rs** built. Today, the best V-Spec II examples sell for over $500,000.

---

## 2008: The R35 — Godzilla Goes Global

The **R35 GT-R** arrived in America in **July 2008** priced at just under $78,000. A **3.8-litre twin-turbo V6** produced **480 horsepower** (later 600 hp in Nismo trim). 0 to 60 mph in **2.7 seconds**. It was faster around the Nürburgring than the Porsche 911 Turbo and Ferrari 599 — at a fraction of the price.

Production officially ended on **August 26, 2025**, with the final example in iconic **Midnight Purple**. Approximately **48,000 R35 GT-Rs** were built worldwide.

## Why the Skyline GT-R Is a Legend

The Skyline GT-R became a legend because it proved that engineering intelligence could beat raw power, that technology could defeat money, and that a car from Japan could outrun anything from Europe.

It won in racing before anyone believed it could. It became immortal through video games. It became a movie icon. And for over 50 years, it carried a single, simple promise: get in, press the throttle, and hold on.

That is the story of Godzilla.
