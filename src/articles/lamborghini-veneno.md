*Built to Celebrate 50 Years of [Lamborghini](/2026/03/01/lamborghini-history/). Brutal, Extreme, and Completely Uncompromising.*

---

In 2013, Lamborghini turned 50 years old. Most car manufacturers mark anniversaries with special edition paint colours, commemorative badges, or limited-run accessories. Lamborghini built the Veneno — a 750 horsepower hypercar so extreme in its design that it looked less like a road car and more like a fighter jet that had accidentally grown wheels.

They made three of them. Then they made nine roadsters. And they charged $4.5 million per car.

Every single one sold immediately.

---

## The Name

"Veneno" means "venom" or "poison" in Spanish. It is also the name of one of the most aggressive fighting bulls in history — a bull so dangerous it killed the matador Manuel García Espartero in 1879 during a corrida in Seville.

Lamborghini has a long tradition of naming its cars after famous fighting bulls — Murciélago, Gallardo, [Aventador](/2026/01/15/lamborghini-aventador-svj/), Diablo. The Veneno continues that tradition at its most extreme.

---

## Why It Exists

The Veneno was created for a single purpose: to demonstrate what Lamborghini's engineers and designers could do when every constraint of cost, regulation, and practicality was removed.

It was designed to be the most visually and technically extreme expression of Lamborghini's capabilities at a specific moment in time — and to celebrate half a century of building cars that other manufacturers considered impossible, starting with the [Miura](/2026/03/01/lamborghini-miura/) in 1966.

---

## The Design — Functional Extremism

Every surface serves an [aerodynamic](/2026/02/12/active-aerodynamics-explained/) function. The massive front splitter generates downforce at high speeds. The side air intakes channel cooling air to the engine and brakes. The enormous fixed rear wing creates downforce exceeding 1,000 Newtons at 300 km/h.

The body panels are made entirely from [carbon fibre](/2026/02/12/hypercar-materials-technology/) monocoque construction. Every gram was accounted for. The result is a car that weighs 1,450 kg despite producing 750 horsepower through a naturally aspirated V12.

## The Engine — The Last Pure Lamborghini V12

The Veneno uses Lamborghini's 6.5L naturally aspirated V12 — the same fundamental architecture that powered the [Aventador](/2026/01/15/lamborghini-aventador-svj/), refined to 750 horsepower at 8,400 RPM. This engine is one of the last great naturally aspirated V12s in production. The sound it produces approaching redline is an acoustic experience that no turbocharged or electrified powertrain replicates.

The 7-speed ISR gearbox completes gear changes in approximately 50 milliseconds — faster than any conventional manual and competitive with modern dual-clutch transmissions.

---

## The Coupe — Three Cars, Three Owners

The original Veneno coupe was unveiled at the 2013 Geneva Motor Show. Three production examples were built — one in white, one in grey, and one in red. Each was allocated before the car was publicly shown, at $3.9 million per car. One coupe is displayed at the Lamborghini Museum in Sant'Agata Bolognese — technically owned by the Vatican City State.

---

## The Roadster — Nine Cars, Open Sky

In 2014, nine Roadsters were produced, each in a different colour. The V12 at full throttle with no roof is described as physically overwhelming. A Roadster sold at RM Sotheby's in 2019 for $8.28 million — making it one of the most [valuable modern collectibles](/2026/02/12/supercar-investment-guide/).

---

## Legacy — What the Veneno Proved

The Veneno proved that Lamborghini's design language could be pushed beyond what anyone thought possible. It established a template for Lamborghini's subsequent limited editions: the [Sián](/2026/03/03/lamborghini-sian/) (first hybrid Lamborghini), the Centenario, and the Invencible all owe something to what the Veneno proved possible.

Today, the V12 lineage continues in the [Revuelto](/2026/03/01/lamborghini-revuelto/) — 1,001hp with hybrid electric power — but the Veneno remains the most extreme expression of Lamborghini's naturally aspirated philosophy.

Twelve cars. Twelve owners. One of the most extraordinary road cars in history.
