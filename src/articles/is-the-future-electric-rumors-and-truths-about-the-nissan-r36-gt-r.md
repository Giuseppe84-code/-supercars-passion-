## What we actually know, what’s been reported, and what’s still speculation

The R35 GT-R is officially over—Nissan built roughly 48,000 units across an 18-year run, with the final car leaving the Tochigi line in late August 2025.  
That means the inevitable question is no longer “if” the next GT-R arrives—but **what it will be**.

Will the so-called **R36** be fully electric? Hybrid? Something else? And can it keep the “Godzilla” soul in an era of batteries and software?

Let’s separate **confirmed facts** from **credible reporting** and **internet noise**, using official concept material and recent executive quotes.

---

## 1) What’s confirmed: the R35 era ended, and the GT-R name will continue

From Nissan’s own farewell statement: the R35 has ended production, and the company positions the GT-R story as continuing beyond this chapter.

This matters because it sets the baseline: **there will be a successor**, but details are still being finalized publicly.

---

## 2) The official “clue” car: Hyper Force concept

If you want the most direct “official design language + tech direction” hint, it’s the **Nissan Hyper Force** concept revealed at Japan Mobility Show.

What Nissan says the concept represents:

* An **all-electric, high-performance** vision aimed at combining daily usability with extreme performance.
* Dual driving modes (e.g., “R” and “GT”) and a gamer/track-oriented cockpit concept.

A lot of enthusiasts treat Hyper Force as “the R36 preview,” but the honest take is:  
**Hyper Force is a direction**, not a production confirmation.

---

## 3) What credible reporting suggests: hybrid is now the leading plan

In April 2025, multiple reputable outlets reported a major shift away from a pure EV GT-R successor toward **an electrified hybrid** approach:

* **The Drive** reported that Nissan’s product planning leadership said the next GT-R is planned as a **hybrid**, with a timeframe stretching toward the **end of the decade**.
* **Car and Driver** similarly reported a next-gen GT-R in **3 to 5 years** with **hybrid power**, emphasizing it would remain turbocharged while adding electrons.
* **MotorTrend** echoed the theme: electrified, but not a pure EV—partly tied to the expectation that a GT-R must be able to deliver repeatable high-performance running (the “record-chasing” logic).
* **Motor1** also reported leadership interest in a GT-R that stays authentic and has a performance mission (including laps at Nürburgring Nordschleife).

### Why hybrid makes sense (even if you *want* EV)

A pure EV supercar is brutally fast once—but sustained performance is the challenge: heat management, battery drain under repeated hot laps, and consistency. That’s exactly the kind of problem a hybrid system can “cover” better today.

So the most realistic “truth” is:

* **Hybrid is the most credible direction right now** (based on 2025 executive quotes via major outlets).

---

## 4) What’s *not* confirmed: the exact powertrain, platform, and launch date

Even with the hybrid-heavy reporting, late 2025 coverage shows uncertainty inside Nissan about the final form:

* **Motor1 (Nov 2025)** reported that Nissan has **no clear plan** yet—combustion, hybrid, or electric are still being evaluated publicly.
* CarThrottle (Nov 2025) similarly pointed to exec comments about “exploring different routes.”
* Carscoops (Aug 2025) also described the successor as not yet finalized on EV vs hybrid.

So here’s the honest framing:

### The “truth” today

* **GT-R will return** (strongly signaled by Nissan messaging).
* **Hybrid appears to be the front-runner** (strong reporting in April 2025).
* **But the final configuration is still fluid** in late 2025 reporting.

---

## 5) Will it keep the GT-R “soul”? Here’s what that likely means

When people say “keep the soul,” they usually mean:

1. **All-weather traction + violence**  
   A GT-R should feel like it can deploy huge power without being precious.
2. **Systems that make you faster**  
   The R35 identity was always “technology as performance.”
3. **A performance mission, not just a halo EV**  
   Nissan leadership has implied the successor must be authentic and worthy of the badge.

If Nissan goes hybrid, the best-case “R36 soul” recipe looks like:

* a turbocharged core (continuity) + electric torque fill (modern edge)
* AWD with smarter torque vectoring
* consistent thermal performance for repeated hard driving

---

## 6) The best way to read R36 “leaks” without getting fooled

A quick filter your readers can use:

* If it references **official Nissan concept material** (Hyper Force), it’s directionally useful.
* If it references **named executives + major outlets**, it’s credible reporting (still not a guarantee).
* If it’s “710 hp confirmed” from social posts with no source, treat it as entertainment.

---

## Bottom line

Right now, the smartest conclusion is:

* The **R36 is likely electrified**, and **hybrid** is the strongest reported direction.
* The **Hyper Force** shows Nissan’s EV performance ambitions, but it doesn’t lock the R36 into full electric.
* The exact timeline and final recipe remain unsettled in late-2025 reporting.
