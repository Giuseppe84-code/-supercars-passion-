When McLaren revealed its MCL-HY Le Mans hypercar earlier this month, most of the headlines went to the race car and its 2027 World Endurance Championship campaign. But hiding alongside it was something arguably even more tantalising for collectors: the **MCL-HY GTR**, a track-only derivative that takes the racing car's DNA and reworks it for a tiny circle of private owners. Only around 30 to 35 will ever be built.

### The road racer, reimagined for the track

The standard MCL-HY is bound by the LMDh rulebook, which means a twin-turbo 2.9-litre V6 paired with a mandated hybrid system. The GTR throws that rulebook out. McLaren has deliberately simplified the formula by dropping the FIA-spec hybrid system entirely, leaving the 2.9-litre twin-turbocharged race engine to do all the work on its own. The result is roughly 730bhp sent to the rear wheels, a lower dry weight, and — crucially — far less operational complexity than a full-house Le Mans prototype.

### Why ditching the hybrid is the smart move

It might seem strange to remove technology from a flagship, but the logic is sound. Le Mans-spec hybrid systems are extraordinarily demanding to run: they need specialist engineers, careful energy management, and constant attention. For a private owner who wants to enjoy their car at track days rather than fielding a professional race team, that is a headache, not a feature. By going non-hybrid, McLaren delivers what it calls an authentic hypercar experience without overwhelming owners with race-level operational demands. In other words: all of the thrill, far less of the hassle.

### More than just a car — the Project: Endurance programme

The GTR is sold through McLaren's Project: Endurance programme, and ownership comes wrapped in an experience money can rarely buy. Owners get behind-the-scenes access to McLaren's WEC operation, development updates as the race car evolves, a presence at the 2027 24 Hours of Le Mans, and participation in a series of track events spread over two years. It is less a purchase than an invitation into McLaren's racing inner sanctum — the kind of access that turns a wealthy enthusiast into an insider.

### A modern echo of the F1 GTR

There is a beautiful symmetry here. Thirty years ago, the McLaren F1 GTR took a road-car-derived racer and put privateer owners in the seat of a genuine Le Mans winner. The MCL-HY GTR consciously revives that idea: a customer track car directly tied to McLaren's outright Le Mans challenger. For a marque chasing motorsport's Triple Crown, it is the perfect way to bring its most devoted clients along for the ride.

### The bigger picture

The GTR underlines something important about where the hypercar world is heading. As race regulations push manufacturers toward ever more complex hybrid systems, there is clearly still huge appetite — and a healthy business case — for stripped-back, engine-led machines built purely for driving pleasure. McLaren is betting that the sound and feel of a naturally responsive twin-turbo V6, unburdened by hybrid complexity, is exactly what its most committed customers want. On the evidence of a 35-car run that will almost certainly sell out, that bet looks safe.
